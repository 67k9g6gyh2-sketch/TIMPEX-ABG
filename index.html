      Sudden desaturation, high pressures or low BP? DOPES checklist
      
    
        
  1. First: disconnect from the ventilator and bag with 100% O₂. This lets stacked breaths out and takes the ventilator out of the equation.
    2. 
        
  2. D · Displaced tube: tube depth at the teeth or lips, ETCO₂ waveform, chest rise and breath sounds on both sides (right mainstem?).
    3. 
        
  3. O · Obstruction: pass a suction catheter; look for kinks, biting, a mucus plug or secretions.
    4. 
        
  4. P · Pneumothorax and other lung causes (bronchospasm, pulmonary edema, collapse, embolism): unequal breath sounds, ultrasound (no lung sliding), chest X-ray; decompress a tension pneumothorax.
    5. 
        
  5. E · Equipment: disconnection, leak, O₂ supply, ventilator fault. If bagging fixes it, suspect the machine or circuit.
    6. 
        
  6. S · Stacked breaths (auto-PEEP), mainly asthma or COPD: disconnect, press on the chest to empty it, then a lower rate and a longer expiration.
    7. 
      

      
Pressures point the way. Peak up, plateau normal: airway or tube (O, bronchospasm). Peak and plateau both up: lung, pleura or chest wall (pneumothorax, mainstem tube, edema, collapse, abdominal distension, auto-PEEP). Low pressures or a low VTE: leak, disconnection or a displaced tube.

Acid–base, step by step

      
    
        
  1. pH: acidemia below 7.35, alkalemia above 7.45; "severe" below 7.20 or above 7.60.
    2. 
        
  2. Primary disorder: the value that moves pH that way. pCO2 high = respiratory acidosis, low = respiratory alkalosis. HCO3 low = metabolic acidosis, high = metabolic alkalosis. Both pushing the same way = mixed.
    3. 
        
  3. Compensation: compare the other value with what the primary disorder predicts (table below). Outside the range = a concomitant second disorder.
    4. 
        
  4. Normal pH does not exclude disease. When pH is normal but pCO2 and HCO3 are both abnormal, both possible primary disorders are tested against their rules. If the other value is outside the expected range, it is a mixed disorder (e.g. pH 7.40, pCO2 20, HCO3 12: metabolic acidosis with respiratory alkalosis), not "fully compensated". Chronic respiratory alkalosis is the one disorder that can bring pH fully back; when the numbers fit it, the app says so and still flags that a mixed disorder can look the same.
    5. 
        
  5. Consistency check first. A blood-gas analyzer calculates HCO3 from pH and pCO2 ([H⁺] = 24 × pCO2 ÷ HCO3). If an entered HCO3 differs from the calculated value by more than 2 mmol/L (or more than 15% when the gap is over 1), the acid–base reading is paused: no interpretation, compensation, bicarbonate advice or deficit until the values fit. Leave HCO3 blank (or tap "Use the calculated HCO3") if it came from a separate chemistry sample.
    6. 
        
  6. Interpretation line: severity + primary disorder + "with concomitant …" or "with appropriate respiratory compensation" (metabolic), or acute / chronic / acute-on-chronic (respiratory).
    7. 
        
  7. The traditional label (uncompensated / partially / fully compensated) is shown under the headline for reference; a normal-pH pattern with a second disorder is labelled "mixed", not "fully compensated".
    8. 
      

    


    

      
Expected compensation

      

        
PrimaryExpected

        
          
Metabolic acidosispCO₂ = (1.5 × HCO₃) + 8 ± 2 (Winter's)

          
Metabolic alkalosispCO₂ = (0.7 × HCO₃) + 20 ± 5 (rarely above 55)

          
Respiratory acidosisHCO₃ = 24 + 0.1 × (pCO₂ − 40) acute; 24 + 0.35 × (pCO₂ − 40) chronic; ± 2

          
Respiratory alkalosisHCO₃ = 24 − 0.2 × (40 − pCO₂) acute; 24 − 0.4 × (40 − pCO₂) chronic; ± 2

        
      

      
For respiratory disorders, an HCO₃ between the acute and chronic values reads as acute-on-chronic, or renal compensation still under way (it takes days).

Other formulas

      
    
        
  • P/F ratio = pO₂ ÷ FiO₂ (fraction).
    • 
        
  • A-aDO₂ = PAO₂ − pO₂, with PAO₂ = 713 × FiO₂ − pCO₂ ÷ 0.8. Age-expected upper limit on room air ≈ age ÷ 4 + 4.
    • 
        
  • HCO₃ deficit (mEq) = 0.5 × weight (kg) × (24 − HCO₃). Switch to 0.4 in the deficit card if you use that factor. An estimate only; bicarbonate therapy is debated, and when it is given, part of the deficit is usually given first and the ABG rechecked.
    • 
        
  • HCO₃ check: [H⁺] = 24 × pCO₂ ÷ HCO₃, with [H⁺] in nmol/L = 10(9 − pH). A blank HCO₃ is calculated this way; an entered HCO₃ more than 2 away is flagged.
    • 
        
  • TCO₂ check: TCO₂ ≈ HCO₃ + 0.03 × pCO₂. A TCO₂ more than 3 away is flagged as a likely typo.

Electrolytes (optional)

      
Every value has a status. Normal or abnormal (arrows); extreme: interpreted as entered but flagged "confirm with the laboratory now"; invalid: not a plausible result, not interpreted, and reported; unit not confirmed: not interpreted until the unit is picked; not measured: left blank, never assumed normal. Accepted (extreme) ranges: Na 90–200 (below 110 or above 170 extreme); K 0.5–12 (2.0–8.0); Cl 50–160 (70–135); iCa 0.1–5 mmol/L (0.6–2.0); glucose 1–5000 mg/dL (20–1500); albumin 0.1–6.5 g/dL (below 1.0 extreme; above 6.5 implausible); BUN 0.5–400 mg/dL (2–200); measured osmolality 150–600 (240–400); pH 6.30–8.00 (below 6.80 or above 7.80 extreme). Unresolved problems go to the ⚠️ Input checks block at the top of the copied message.

      
Nothing here is required. Each line appears only when its inputs are entered.

      
    
        
  • Anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO₃). Normal is lab-dependent: 12 ± 4 (8–16) in the classic range, about 3–11 on modern ion-selective analyzers; pick your lab's under the inputs. i-STAT reports the gap with K (Na + K − Cl − TCO₂; reference 10–20), shown in the card only.
    • 
        
  • Albumin-corrected gap = AG + 2.5 × (4.0 − albumin g/dL). Each 1 g/dL fall in albumin lowers the gap by about 2.5, so a low albumin can hide a high-gap acidosis.
    • 
        
  • Delta ratio = (AG − normal mean) ÷ (24 − HCO₃): < 0.4 normal-gap acidosis; 0.4–0.8 combined high- and normal-gap acidosis (or kidney failure); 0.8–2 pure high-gap acidosis (DKA often near 1, lactic near 1.6); > 2 high-gap acidosis plus metabolic alkalosis (or a pre-existing compensated respiratory acidosis). A high gap with a normal or high HCO₃ is read as a hidden high-gap acidosis with metabolic alkalosis.
    • 
        
  • Unexplained gap = (AG − normal mean) − lactate − β-OHB, when those are entered; > 5 points to another anion (kidney failure, toxins, ketones if not measured).
    • 
        
  • Corrected Na (shown when glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL) = Na + 1.6 × (glucose − 100) ÷ 100 (Katz) or 2.4 per 100 (Hillier; fits better above 400 mg/dL).
    • 
        
  • Effective osmolality = 2 Na + glucose ÷ 18. Calculated osmolality adds BUN ÷ 2.8. Osmolal gap = measured − calculated; normal < 10. A high gap with a high anion gap suggests methanol or ethylene glycol; early after ingestion the osmolal gap rises before the anion gap, and late the reverse.
    • 
        
  • DKA (ADA/EASD 2024): glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL or known diabetes (tick the box); β-OHB ≥ 3.0 mmol/L or urine ketones ≥ 2+ (β-OHB is used first when both are entered); pH < 7.3 or HCO₃ < 18, applied exactly as written. When only the pH criterion is met (HCO₃ 18 or more), the app flags that the acidemia is not explained by ketoacidosis alone and that a respiratory acidosis may coexist. Each criterion is met, not met, or not assessed, giving biochemical criteria met, possible, not confirmed (something missing) or not met; the diagnosis itself also needs the bedside picture. Ketoacidosis with glucose < 200 is euglycemic DKA only with known diabetes. Severity (not every finding is needed; the worst one sets the grade): severe if β-OHB > 6.0, pH < 7.00 or HCO₃ < 10; moderate if pH 7.00–7.25 or HCO₃ 10–14.9; otherwise mild (pH 7.26–7.29 or HCO₃ 15–17.9). β-OHB 6.0 exactly is not severe. The finding that set the grade is named. Mental status (alert, drowsy, stupor or coma) is part of the consensus grading and must be assessed at the bedside. The consensus is written for venous samples; arterial pH runs a little higher. The anion gap is no longer a diagnostic or resolution criterion.
    • 
        
  • HHS (ADA/EASD 2024): glucose ≥ 600 mg/dL; calculated effective osmolality (2 Na + glucose ÷ 18) > 300, or calculated total osmolality (adds BUN ÷ 2.8) > 320; no significant ketonemia (β-OHB < 3.0 or urine ketones < 2+); and pH ≥ 7.3 with HCO₃ ≥ 15. The app names which calculation met the osmolality criterion. A measured osmolality is shown as support but never stands in for the calculated values, so with a measured value alone HHS stays "possible" until Na (and BUN) are entered. Without a ketone result it is only "possible". Hyperosmolality with significant ketonemia or acidosis is reported as mixed features, which the consensus advises treating as DKA; DKA with HHS-range values is reported as overlapping DKA–HHS.
    • 
        
  • Potassium: hyperkalemia mild 5.5–5.9, moderate 6.0–6.4, severe ≥ 6.5 (UKKA 2023); hypokalemia mild 3.0–3.4, moderate 2.5–2.9, severe < 2.5. When DKA or HHS treatment is likely and K has not been entered (or is not valid), the app says to check K before any insulin; insulin is held while K < 3.5 (ADA/EASD 2024).
    • 
        
  • Sodium: hyponatremia mild 130–135, moderate 125–129, profound < 125 (European guideline 2014).
    • 
        
  • Units: glucose, ionized Ca, albumin and BUN/urea each need their unit picked. The app never infers a unit from the size of the number, because the two readings can be opposite emergencies (glucose 7 is severe hypoglycemia in mg/dL and normal in mmol/L; iCa 1.6 is high in mmol/L and 0.40 mmol/L in mg/dL; albumin 5 g/L is 0.5 g/dL, not 5 g/dL). Conversions: glucose mmol/L × 18 = mg/dL; iCa mg/dL ÷ 4.008 = mmol/L; albumin g/L ÷ 10 = g/dL; urea mmol/L × 2.8 = BUN mg/dL. When a value is converted, both the typed and the standard value are shown.
    • 
        
  • Arrows: Na 135–145, K 3.5–5.0, Cl 98–107, iCa 1.12–1.32 mmol/L, albumin < 3.5 g/dL, measured osmolality 275–295, glucose 70–180 mg/dL (the inpatient target ceiling).

Oxygenation grade

      
    
        
  • Room air: normal ≥ 80, mild hypoxemia 60–79, moderate 40–59, severe < 40 mmHg. Over age 60, subtract 1 mmHg per year from the normal and mild limits (applied up to age 90).
    • 
        
  • On O₂: uncorrected (below the normal limit), corrected (normal limit to 100), overcorrected (above 100).
    • 
      

    
On oxygen, two things are reported separately: whether the pO2 is adequate on the current support (uncorrected / corrected / above 100), and how impaired gas exchange is relative to FiO2 (P/F ≤ 300 mildly, ≤ 200 moderately, ≤ 100 severely impaired). pO2 105 on FiO2 100% is "above 100 (O2 can be lowered), with moderately impaired oxygenation (P/F 105)", never "normal".

    


    

      
Interpretation in the message

      
    
        
  • Line 1: acid–base reading + oxygenation grade.
    • 
        
  • Traditional label (uncompensated / partially / fully compensated).
    • 
        
  • Respiratory failure: Type 2 if a respiratory acidosis has pCO₂ > 45 (with acidemia if pH < 7.35; "chronic, compensated" if pH ≥ 7.35); otherwise Type 1 if pO₂ < 60.
    • 
        
  • Gas exchange: P/F, then A-aDO₂ against the age norm on room air, or the a/A ratio on oxygen (A-aDO₂ rises with FiO₂; a/A does not). Widened → V/Q mismatch, shunt or diffusion defect; normal with hypoxemia → hypoventilation or low inspired O₂.

Support modes

      
    
        
  • RA / NC: FiO₂ 21%, or 20% + 4% per L/min on nasal cannula. The desired FiO₂ is turned back into a cannula flow; above 44% the app points to a Venturi mask or HFNC.
    • 
        
  • Mask / HFNC: enter the set FiO₂ (Venturi, HFNC) or an estimate (simple mask, non-rebreather). HFNC at ≥ 30 L/min counts for the ARDS P/F criterion.
    • 
        
  • NIV: BiPAP (IPAP/EPAP, pressure support = IPAP − EPAP) or CPAP. EPAP ≥ 5 counts for the ARDS P/F criterion. Hypercapnic patients on NIV: SpO₂ 88–92%; if still acidotic, raise IPAP toward 20–30 cmH₂O, fix leak, recheck the ABG in 1–2 h. BTS/ICS 2016. Hypoxemic failure: HFNC is suggested over NIV. ERS 2022.
    • 
        
  • Ventilator: choose the mode (ACVC, ACPC, PRVC, SIMV, PS), then enter the present settings and what the ventilator shows. Only the fields that apply to the mode are used. The copied message follows your intubated layout: settings, pressures, PBW, oxygenation index, mechanics and a ventilator interpretation (see "Reading the ventilator").
    • 
        
  • Target pCO₂ on the ventilator: with P/F ≤ 300 the ARDSNet rule comes first (pH 7.30–7.45 accepted; pH 7.15–7.30: aim for pH 7.30 but not below pCO₂ 25; pH < 7.15: rate up to 35, using the Winter's value in metabolic acidosis, then VT +1 mL/kg steps if pH stays < 7.15). The current rate is Ftotal when entered (what the patient actually breathes), otherwise BUR. With auto-PEEP or a short expiratory time, the app does not advise a faster rate. Otherwise the Winter's value in metabolic acidosis, expected compensation in metabolic alkalosis, or the pCO₂ that gives pH 7.40 (7.38 for chronic hypercapnia) at the current HCO₃. RR needed = RR × pCO₂ ÷ target, assuming VT and dead space stay the same. Projected pH = 9 − log10(24 × target pCO₂ ÷ HCO₃), assuming HCO₃ does not change. When the ARDSNet pH goal cannot be reached even at the pCO₂ floor of 25 (e.g. pH 7.20, pCO₂ 26, HCO₃ 10 → ≈ 7.22 at pCO₂ 25), the app says so instead of offering a target. With pH < 7.20 a faster rate is suggested only with the plateau, auto-PEEP and expiratory-time checks spelled out. Set rate vs total rate: a new set rate is calculated only when the breaths are machine-delivered (Ftotal within 2 of the set rate, or Ftotal not entered, which is stated). When the patient triggers extra breaths, or takes spontaneous breaths in SIMV, the app gives the change in alveolar and minute ventilation needed instead, because raising the set rate may only replace the patient's own breaths; reassess effort, synchrony, VT and measured minute ventilation first. Ftotal below the set rate is flagged as an entry error and no rate is calculated. A typed (custom) target gets the same projected pH and the same checks. No rate change is suggested if it would move the pH away from normal and leave it outside 7.35–7.45 (for example a custom pCO₂ 25 at pH 7.40, or lowering the rate toward the Winter's value when pCO₂ is below it). These are theoretical estimates: they assume VT, dead space and CO₂ production stay the same and that the set rate is the delivered rate, and they ignore the extra dead-space ventilation and shorter expiration that a faster rate brings. Recheck the ABG after any change.
    • 
        
  • Target pO₂ for the desired FiO₂ (Auto): 104 − 0.4 × age (your sheet); pO₂ 60 when there is CO₂ retention (box ticked, or respiratory acidosis with pCO₂ > 45 when not ventilated), matching SpO₂ 88–92%; pO₂ 65 when ventilated with P/F ≤ 300 (ARDSNet goal 55–80). If pO₂ is already adequate or inside the target window (55–65 for CO₂ retention, 55–80 for ARDSNet), the app says "keep" instead of changing the oxygen. Choose Custom to override.
    • 
        
  • Ventilator goals (ARDSNet): VT 6 mL/kg PBW (4–8), plateau ≤ 30, pH 7.30–7.45, RR ≤ 35, pO₂ 55–80 or SpO₂ 88–95% in ARDS. Without ARDS, SSC 2026 suggests 6–8 mL/kg PBW. Oxygen targets outside ARDS: UK-ROX (JAMA 2025) found no mortality difference between SpO₂ 90% and usual care; avoid unnecessary hyperoxia.
    • 
      

    


    

      
Reading the ventilator

      
    
        
  • Volume: VT per kg predicted body weight uses VTE (exhaled) when entered, else the set TV. Lung-protective 4–8 mL/kg, 6 in ARDS. ARDSNet.
    • 
        
  • Rate: Ftotal above BUR means the patient is triggering extra breaths. Ftotal = BUR means no own breaths (sedated, paralyzed, low drive, or over-ventilated). In metabolic acidosis, match the patient's own rate before sedating or paralyzing.
    • 
        
  • Peak and plateau: plateau (inspiratory hold) is the alveolar pressure; keep ≤ 30. High peak with high plateau = stiff lung or chest wall (ARDS, edema, pneumonia, atelectasis, mainstem intubation, pneumothorax, effusion, abdominal distension). High peak with normal plateau = airway problem (bronchospasm, secretions, kinked or bitten tube). Peak − plateau is normally ≤ 5–10.
    • 
        
  • Driving pressure = plateau − PEEP (total PEEP if auto-PEEP was measured); ≤ 15 is the usual limit. Amato, NEJM 2015. Contradictory pressures are flagged and the values that depend on them withheld: a plateau above the peak or below PEEP (no plateau-based advice; repeat the hold, about 0.5 s, with the patient passive), total PEEP below set PEEP (no auto-PEEP, driving pressure or compliance), measured PEEP more than 2 away from set PEEP (leak or active breathing), and a plateau taken while the patient triggers breaths (reliable only when passive). Only a plateau (inspiratory hold) is used: without one, driving pressure and static compliance are shown as not available. ΔPinsp and peak pressure are not substitutes (ΔPinsp equals driving pressure only if flow reaches zero before the breath ends). A ventilator-measured Cstat is shown with its source.
    • 
        
  • Static compliance = VT ÷ driving pressure (normal about 40–100 mL/cmH₂O when intubated). Dynamic compliance = VT ÷ (peak − PEEP).
    • 
        
  • Airway resistance = (peak − plateau) ÷ flow in L/s, valid with constant (square-wave) flow in volume control; about 5–10 cmH₂O/L/s with a tube, > 15 high.
    • 
        
  • Air trapping: auto-PEEP = total PEEP (expiratory hold) − set PEEP. Time constant τ = resistance × compliance; expiration needs about 3τ. Expiratory time = (60 ÷ rate) × E ÷ (I + E).
    • 
        
  • Leak: (VTi − VTE) ÷ VTi; 10% or more suggests a cuff, circuit or chest-tube leak.
    • 
        
  • Minute ventilation = VT × rate; predicted 100 mL/kg PBW per min. Ventilatory ratio = (VE in mL/min × pCO₂) ÷ (PBW × 100 × 37.5); about 1 is normal, higher means more dead space or CO₂ production. Sinha, AJRCCM 2019.
    • 
        
  • Oxygenation index = FiO₂ × mean airway pressure × 100 ÷ pO₂. Shown as a number to trend. The severity bands for OI and OSI (OI 4–16 / ≥ 16; OSI 5–12 / ≥ 12) come from PALICC-2 and are for children; there are no validated adult bands, so none are applied. Emeriaud, Pediatr Crit Care Med 2023.
    • 
        
  • Mechanical power (J/min): volume control 0.098 × RR × VT (L) × (peak − ½ × driving pressure); pressure control 0.098 × RR × VT × (ΔPinsp + PEEP). Above 17 J/min was linked to higher mortality. Serpa Neto, ICM 2018; Chiumello, Crit Care 2020.
    • 
        
  • PEEP/FiO₂ tables (ARDSNet), shown when P/F ≤ 300. Higher PEEP without prolonged recruitment maneuvers is suggested in moderate–severe ARDS (ATS 2024); ESICM 2023 made no recommendation either way.
    • 
        
  • RSBI on pressure support = rate ÷ VT (L). Shown for information only: it is never a gate for a breathing trial or extubation. The 2024 AARC guideline suggests that calculating RSBI is not needed to decide readiness for a spontaneous breathing trial (conditional, moderate certainty). Roberts, Respir Care 2024; Yang & Tobin, NEJM 1991.
    • 
        
  • Mindray SV-series layout: modes as on the screen (V-A/C, P-A/C, PRVC, CPAP/PSV, V-SIMV, P-SIMV, PRVC-SIMV; DuoLevel and APRV not covered), settings by their screen names (O2%, TV, ΔPinsp, f or fsimv, Tinsp, PEEP with OFF = 0, ΔPsupp, F-Trig, Tslope, Exp%), and the measured values in the order of the Values screen. When entered, the ventilator's own MV, Ri, Cstat, Cdyn and RCexp are used: measured MV for minute ventilation and the ventilatory ratio; Ri when peak − plateau cannot give resistance (pressure modes); RCexp as the time constant for the air-trapping check. A calculated Cstat that differs from the ventilator's by more than 25% is flagged (the plateau may not come from a true hold).
    • 
        
  • Tinsp, I:E and expiratory time: I:E is worked out at the set rate (as the ventilator shows it); the expiratory time actually available uses the total rate, because extra breaths shorten it.
    • 
        
  • SIMV: mandatory (fmand) and spontaneous (fspn, TVe spn, MVspn) breaths are reported separately; the spontaneous VT is checked against 4–8 mL/kg too. Pressure support at or above Pinsp means the mandatory breaths add little. In SIMV, TVi and TVe on the screen can come from different breaths, so their difference is not read as a leak; MVleak is used instead. For liberation, daily spontaneous breathing trials beat stepping down the SIMV rate: weaning failure 42% with SIMV vs 23% with pressure support (Brochard, AJRCCM 1994), and a once-daily trial was faster than SIMV (Esteban, NEJM 1995).
    • 
        
  • RSBI from the screen is a weaning index only on minimal support (PS ≤ 8 or CPAP); on higher support, and in SIMV where it counts the spontaneous breaths only, it is shown but not used.
    • 
        
  • PEEP 0 (OFF): in ICU patients without ARDS, lower PEEP (0–5) was noninferior to PEEP 8 for ventilator-free days (RELAx, JAMA 2020). With ARDS-range oxygenation the PEEP/FiO₂ tables apply instead.
    • 
        
  • Fill from a photo (optional AI): a photo of the Values screen is sent with your own Anthropic API key, and the numbers come back into the boxes, outlined until you check or edit them. Nothing is sent unless you choose a photo there.
    • 
        
  • Entry checks: a TV, VTE or VTi of 2 or less is read as litres (0.45 → 450 mL); a height of 1.2–2.3 as metres and 48–90 as inches. The app flags Ftotal below the set rate, a plateau above the peak, VTE ≥ 20% above the set TV in volume control (stacked breaths or a sensor error), and VTE ≥ 15% below it (leak).
    • 
        
  • Ti and Te without an I:E: in pressure control from the set Ti; in volume control Ti ≈ TV ÷ peak flow, which assumes square-wave flow and no inspiratory pause (a decelerating ramp or a pause makes Ti longer). This lets the app check expiratory time against 3 time constants.
    • 
        
  • ARDS oxygenation criterion (2023 global definition): P/F ≤ 300 or SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 315 (SpO₂ ≤ 97%) on HFNO ≥ 30 L/min, NIV/CPAP ≥ 5 cmH₂O, or invasive ventilation. Above SpO₂ 97% the SpO₂/FiO₂ ratio is not valid for grading; if a P/F assessment is needed, get an ABG rather than changing oxygen to make the ratio usable. Severity (mild 200–300 / S/F 235–315, moderate 100–200 / 148–235, severe ≤ 100 / ≤ 148) is graded only for intubated patients; the app also requires PEEP ≥ 5 before grading, because sources differ on whether the global definition keeps the Berlin PEEP rule. Meeting the oxygenation criterion is not a diagnosis: ARDS also needs bilateral opacities (or ultrasound findings) within 1 week of a known risk, not fully explained by heart failure or fluid overload. Matthay, AJRCCM 2024. Oxygen saturation index = FiO₂ × mean airway pressure × 100 ÷ SpO₂ (trend only; no adult bands).
    • 
        
  • Drive and effort (a breathing patient): P0.1, the airway pressure drop 100 ms into an occluded breath. > 3.5 cmH₂O detected high effort (80% sensitive, 77% specific); ≤ 1.0 detected low effort (100% sensitive, 92% specific). Telias, AJRCCM 2020. Values displayed by different ventilators are not interchangeable. ΔPocc: the largest drop below PEEP during an end-expiratory hold over one effort. Pmus ≈ ¾ × |ΔPocc| (> 10 high, > 15 excessive); dynamic transpulmonary driving pressure ≈ (peak − PEEP) + ⅔ × |ΔPocc| (> 15 high, > 20 excessive). Bertoni, Crit Care 2019. ΔPocc can underestimate effort when auto-PEEP is present. When lung and diaphragm protection conflict, the lung comes first. Goligher, AJRCCM 2020.
    • 
        
  • Weaning screen (shown on low support with a gas or SpO₂ and a breathing patient): P/F ≥ 150 (or SpO₂ > 90% on FiO₂ ≤ 40%), PEEP ≤ 8, FiO₂ ≤ 40–50%, rate ≤ 35, VT > 5 mL/kg, pH ≥ 7.30, P0.1 not high (RSBI is listed for information but never counted). These are indicators, not prerequisites; the clinical items (cause improving, stable BP, awake, cough, secretions) are the user's to judge. SBT: 30 min on PS 5–8 gave more successful extubations than 2 h of T-piece (82% vs 74%). Subirà, JAMA 2019. Pressure augmentation for the first SBT (conditional) and preventive NIV after extubation for high-risk patients (strong). ACCP/ATS 2017. NIV over HFNC when at high risk of extubation failure; HFNC over conventional O₂ when at low risk. ERS 2022. Cuff-leak test if at high risk of stridor, with steroids if the leak is small. ATS/CHEST 2017. SBTs with or without low-level PS (≤ 8), and no FiO₂ increase during the trial. AARC 2024.
    • 
      

      
Measure plateau and total PEEP with a short hold in a passive patient; active breathing makes them unreliable.

    


    

      
Ventilator tab and scalars

      
    
        
  • Separate assessment: the Ventilator tab reads the ventilator on its own, with the same inputs as MV in the ABG tab (they carry over). When the ABG tab's support is set to MV, its gas is linked for P/F, oxygenation index, ventilatory ratio and the rate target.
    • 
        
  • Scalars: add a photo of the ventilator screen. It is shown only on your phone (never uploaded or saved). Compare it with the schematic drawings for your mode and tap Seen; findings feed the interpretation, the suggestions and both messages. The drawings are schematic, not to scale.
    • 
        
  • Flow–time: expiratory flow not returning to zero → auto-PEEP; small expiratory bumps without a breath → ineffective efforts (often over-assistance with hyperinflation); two breaths with no exhalation between → double triggering (stacked volume); sawtooth → secretions or circuit water; low, long concave expiratory tail → flow limitation; abrupt end of inspiratory flow with a dip or second breath → early cycling. Tobin ch. 53; Brochard & Lellouche ch. 8; Leatherman ch. 30.
    • 
        
  • Pressure–time: concave (scalloped) rise in volume control → flow starvation; big dip before the breath → trigger delay or auto-PEEP; breaths with no dip, fast and regular → auto-triggering (leak, water, cardiac oscillation, sensitive trigger); end-inspiratory spike on PS → delayed cycling; initial spike → rise time too fast; slow rounded rise → too slow; mid-breath dip or a stacked breath after a machine breath → reverse triggering. Tobin ch. 53; Mancebo ch. 6; Brochard & Lellouche ch. 8; Akoumianaki, Chest 2013.
    • 
        
  • Automatic reading: drag a box around each tracing. The app finds the trace by its colour (grid, numbers, axes and glare are greyish and ignored), follows it across the box one pixel column at a time, finds the zero-flow axis (allowing for a tilted photo; otherwise the level where inspired and expired volumes balance) and the PEEP baseline, then splits the trace into breaths and tests each breath. Taps are optional corrections: tap the trace to re-pick its colour, or tap the zero/PEEP line. A small flow blip that lines up with a pressure breath is counted as a delivered breath, not a missed effort. Flow: expiratory flow still below zero and still returning when the next breath starts (auto-PEEP); a bump toward zero that falls back without a breath, or a small inspiratory blip (ineffective effort; within the first fifth of expiration = early cycling); an expiration far shorter than usual with most of the volume not exhaled (double triggering); fine oscillation on the expiratory limb (sawtooth); most of expiration spent at 10–45% of peak expiratory flow (obstruction). Pressure: dip before the breath (patient-triggered; deep = trigger delay); no dip on PS (auto-triggering); concave rise in volume control (flow starvation); dip during the machine breath; overshoot, end-inspiratory spike or slow rise in pressure modes; breaths far closer together than usual (double triggering). Each finding is reported as "n of N breaths" with a confidence, and marked on the photo.
    • 
        
  • Limits: one photo, no time scale unless you enter the seconds shown; screen glare, a tilted photo or overlapping curves reduce accuracy (the "line found in %" figure tells you). Anything not listed is left to your eye. Tested on simulated screens: every pattern above detected, no false findings on 600 normal tracings. Always check the red marks against the photo.
    • 
        
  • AI read (optional): sends the photo, the mode and the pattern definitions above to Anthropic's Claude using your own API key, as a second opinion. It is the only feature that sends data off the phone; the key is kept on the phone only if you tick "Remember".
    • 
        
  • Volume–time: expiratory volume ending on a flat plateau above zero and then reset → leak (cuff, circuit, bronchopleural fistula); volume still falling when the next breath starts → air trapping; one breath far larger than the rest → stacking (double triggering). The reader checks these on the volume tracing when you box it. Tobin ch. 53.
    • 
        
  • Shaded and two-colour waveforms (Mindray): the reader follows the edge of the shaded area, not its middle. When breaths are shaded in two colours it counts each colour and its width at half height; in SIMV the colour whose width matches Tinsp is the mandatory breath.
    • 
        
  • Stress index (volume control, passive machine-timed breaths with square-wave flow): the shape of the pressure rise, fitted as P = a·tb + c after the first quarter of inflation. b > 1.1 (upward concavity) suggests tidal overdistension; b < 0.9 (downward concavity) suggests tidal recruitment; ≈ 1 is linear. In patient-triggered breaths the same concave shape is read as flow starvation instead. A stiff chest wall can raise the index; treat a photo estimate as a prompt to look at the ventilator. Ranieri, Anesthesiology 2000; Terragni, Anesthesiology 2013.
    • 
        
  • DOPES (the card at the top of the Ventilator tab) for sudden deterioration: Displaced tube, Obstruction, Pneumothorax, Equipment failure, Stacked breaths; disconnect and bag first.
    • 
        
  • Severe airflow obstruction (air trapping, obstructive flow or high resistance with normal compliance): aim for plateau < 30 (≤ 25 ideal) and pH ≥ 7.2. Plateau > 30 → less minute ventilation; pH < 7.2 with plateau < 25 → more; pH < 7.2 with plateau 25–30 → no change. The app does not advise a faster rate while air trapping is present. Leatherman, Tobin ch. 30.
    • 
        
  • Cycle-off on PS: COPD often needs a higher cycle-off (around 50% or more of peak flow) to avoid delayed cycling and missed triggers; stiff, restrictive lungs often do better with a low threshold. Brochard & Lellouche ch. 8.
    • 
      

      
Confirm a waveform finding at the bedside (look at the patient; an inspiratory or expiratory hold) before changing settings.

    


    

      
Suggestions and their sources

      
    
        
  • O₂ target SpO₂ 94–98%, or 88–92% if at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure or the gas shows respiratory-acidosis hypercapnia. Stop O₂ once the target holds on room air. BTS guideline for oxygen use in adults, 2017.
    • 
        
  • NIV for acidotic hypercapnia in a COPD exacerbation (pCO₂ ≥ 45 and pH ≤ 7.35) that persists after optimal medical therapy; pH ≤ 7.25 is life-threatening. GOLD 2026 report; NICE QS10. BTS/ICS uses pH < 7.35 with pCO₂ > 6.5 kPa (≈ 49 mmHg) after initial medical therapy, and SpO₂ 88–92% on NIV. BTS/ICS 2016.
    • 
        
  • HFNC over conventional oxygen and over NIV in acute hypoxemic failure. ERS 2022. In sepsis, HFNC when P/F < 200 or SpO₂/FiO₂ < 235. SSC 2026.
    • 
        
  • O₂ on the ventilator: a conservative SpO₂ target of 90% did not lower 90-day mortality. UK-ROX, JAMA 2025. SSC 2026 suggests either a lower (about 90–93%) or a higher (≥ 96%) target in sepsis. Avoid hyperoxia.
    • 
        
  • Bicarbonate: suggested against for hypoperfusion-induced lactic acidemia; suggested (very low certainty) for septic shock with pH ≤ 7.2 and AKI stage 2–3. Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026. No mortality benefit in severe metabolic acidemia with AKI. BICARICU-2, JAMA 2025. In DKA only if pH < 7.0. ADA/EASD/JBDS hyperglycaemic crises consensus 2024.
    • 
        
  • Lactate: measure, repeat to guide resuscitation, capillary refill time as an adjunct; ≥ 30 mL/kg crystalloid in 3 h for sepsis-induced hypoperfusion. SSC 2026.
    • 
        
  • ARDS: P/F ≤ 300 (or SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 315) with bilateral opacities not explained by heart failure or fluid overload. Global definition, 2023. Lung-protective ventilation, early prone positioning for moderate–severe ARDS (> 12 h/day ATS, > 16 h/day ESICM), corticosteroids and higher PEEP suggested; no prolonged recruitment maneuvers. ATS 2024; ESICM 2023.
    • 
        
  • Metabolic alkalosis: treat the cause; IV 0.9% saline for chloride-responsive alkalosis, K⁺ and Mg replacement. MSD Manual.
    • 
        
  • With electrolytes: high- vs normal-gap causes (Kraut & Madias 2007); DKA fluids 500–1000 mL/h for 2–4 h, insulin 0.1 U/kg/h once K > 3.5, dextrose when glucose < 250 mg/dL, bicarbonate only if pH < 7.0; HHS fluids first, glucose fall ≤ 90–120 mg/dL/h and Na ≤ 10 mmol/L per 24 h (ADA/EASD 2024); hyperkalemia ECG, IV calcium for ECG changes, insulin–glucose from 6.0, salbutamol from 6.5 (UKKA 2023); severe symptomatic hyponatremia 150 mL 3% saline over 20 min, rise ≤ 10 mmol/L in 24 h (European guideline 2014); toxic alcohols: fomepizole or ethanol, early hemodialysis, poison centre.
    • 
        
  • Lines without a source are standard physiology (e.g. Winter's range breaches, causes of hyperventilation).
    • 
      

      
Suggestions are prompts to check against the patient and your institution's protocols, not orders.
